## Result: autonomous arrival at a legal sweet spot (`approach_door`, run `ap_1`)
The robot started with the door behind-right (about 3.42 m by tape, about -130 deg), spun right to find it, then repeated: measure the door (median of 5 frames: bearing from the entrance detector, outline area) -> turn a short pulse until the bearing is within +/- 4 deg, or drive a 0.6 s burst at v = 0.85 -> stop, hold 0.8 s, measure again. Range = 3.42 m * sqrt(A0 / A) with A0 the outline area of the first sighting (the only manual number is that tape value). It stopped at an estimated 1.97 m after 51 steps, 10 drive bursts, about 2.5 minutes, no human input during the run.
**Tape afterwards: 1.67 m, pointing at the door centre.** The legal window is 1.5-3.0 m, so this is a usable sweet spot. Range-from-area error +0.30 m (+18 %; the estimate was too long, consistent with the two triangulation runs below). One run, one door, one day.
Observed weakness: about 35 of the 51 steps were turn pulses hunting around the 4 deg window (+8 / -8 deg alternation: the shortest useful pulse turns more than 4 deg). Planned: accept about 8 deg before driving, or a shorter pulse. The "pulse and re-check" scheme itself (never trust the open-loop heading, re-measure after every move) is what made the arrival work and is the candidate strategy for the approach.

### Why not just a better model? (research and offline tests on the same 793 frames, 2026-11-11 evening)
- **Regime, not model:** faces of 22-40 px at the sensor are in the TinyFace regime (6-32 px high, average about 20). Models that exceed 99 % on LFW score only about 50-60 % Rank-1 there (published figures: about 59 % cited in a review, 46-50 % for recent models; super-resolution pipelines reach about 47 % Rank-1, FaceMoE gains about 2.3 points). Close-range face unlock (a laptop or phone at 0.3-0.5 m, 150-300 px faces, frontal, often infrared or depth) is a different regime. Fine-tuning on TinyFace itself is reported to hurt the other resolutions.
- **Offline tests (descriptive; 43 entries, leave-one-member-out, `scripts/d2_entry_level.py`, `scripts/d2_model_compare.py`, ROC AUC where 0.5 = no separation):** frame level buffalo_s 0.710; entry level (mean embedding per entry) 0.735; face width at least 30 px only: 0.63 (frame) / 0.68 (entry), a smaller sample; upsampling the frame x2 before detection and embedding: 0.702 / 0.728 (no gain). At the 0.45 threshold the entry-level mean gives TAR 0.64 at FAR 0.28 (frame level 0.34 at 0.026 with this protocol; the pilot tool reports 0.399 at 0.066): the mean embedding raises both, it does not separate better. **buffalo_l (ResNet50, `w600k_r50`) on the same frames (`scripts/d2_model_compare.py`, `data/d2_model_compare_1111.json`): frame level AUC 0.787, entry level 0.843** (buffalo_s 0.710 / 0.735; x2 upsampling: 0.781 / 0.838): a larger recogniser is worth +0.08 to +0.11 AUC, the largest gain of anything tried tonight, but 0.84 is still far from a usable verifier at 22-53 px (TAR at FAR 1 % not computed for it; 792 of 793 frames embedded, 42 impostor and 78 genuine entry queries). buffalo_l is a stock InsightFace pack (a 282 MB download that stalled twice before finishing), not yet in the deployed pipeline (buffalo_s, chosen for speed): a candidate for HNHL's perception evaluation together with the resolution fix.
- **Training on our three identities would not be a valid fix:** the whitelist is arbitrary registered occupants, three identities overfit, and the same people are the test. A legitimate training route is a low-resolution recipe on a large public set (resolution augmentation, quality-adaptive margin such as AdaFace) and a lot of compute on an RTX 4050 6 GB for an expected gain of a few points, against a 2.5x pixel gain from the sensor.
- **The cheaper lever is pixels:** the door ESP32 runs `esp32/sentry_cam` at `FRAMESIZE_VGA` (640x480); an OV2640 does hardware JPEG up to UXGA (1600x1200), i.e. up to 2.5x the face width at the same place (22-40 px to 55-100 px, above the pilot's 45 px floor). Cost: frame rate over the 2.4 GHz link (20 MHz clock gave 1.1 fps, 10 MHz gives 9-10 fps at VGA; larger frames are bandwidth-bound), a reflash or the web-UI control (port 80 did not answer tonight). Measured camera-to-door distance 2.11 m (inside 1.5-3.0 m): the face width of 30 px at 2.11 m means a lens of about 77 degrees; a closer or less tilted placement and a narrower lens add pixels too.

### ESP32-CAM performance research (2026-11-11 evening, for the dorm test; web sources, not our measurements)
- **Frame rate falls steeply with frame size.** The only systematic benchmark found (Nowroz et al., arXiv 2505.24081, one AI-Thinker ESP32-CAM, OV2640, JPEG over HTTP/Wi-Fi, 15 min per size; XCLK, JPEG quality, PSRAM, Wi-Fi band and client are NOT stated, so it is a reference, not a prediction): QVGA 44.4 fps, VGA 14.2, SVGA 8.3, XGA 3.4, SXGA 2.3, UXGA 1.3 (file size 15.6 KB at VGA, 67.6 KB at SXGA, 108 KB at UXGA); MCU temperature 70-74 C at every size, no throughput effect; input voltage below about 2.985 V hurts, so a regulated supply matters. Our board at VGA gave 9.1-9.9 fps (firmware note: XCLK 20 MHz gave 1.1 fps through interference, 10 MHz is used), below the paper's 14.2: the link, not the sensor, limits it. Expect about 2 fps at SXGA and about 1.3 fps at UXGA on this link: one walk-in of 3-5 s still gives 5-10 frames.
- **Pixels per face:** SXGA (1280x1024) doubles the VGA face width (22-40 px to about 44-80 px), UXGA 2.5x (about 55-100 px), XGA 1.6x. Community rule of thumb: under 60-80 px poor recognition (not a benchmark).
- **Cheap settings, unmeasured gains:** Wi-Fi sleep off (`WiFi.setSleep(false)`), latest-frame grab and two buffers with PSRAM (our firmware already uses `CAMERA_GRAB_LATEST`, `fb_count` 2), a stable 5 V supply (the tower should use a power bank or adapter, not the laptop port). MJPEG streams serve one client efficiently (known: close every other reader).
- **Optics:** a swapped M12 lens (Instructables "ESP32-Cam M12 Lens Hack"; 6-8 mm lenses sold in sets) narrows the field of view and enlarges faces at the same resolution. Unverified here: the field of view of an M12 lens depends on the sensor size (a printed 69.5 degrees refers to a larger sensor, on the OV2640 it would be narrower), the back-focus of longer lenses and the thread pitch (M12 x 0.5) must match. Not tested for face range by any source found.
- **Other boards (no measured streaming comparison found):** XIAO ESP32S3 Sense (8 MB PSRAM, external antenna, sensor swappable to OV5640, 5 MP), ESP32-S3 camera boards; OV5640 reports of overheating. A new board means new firmware and the spec names OV2640/GC2145.
- **Related projects use the same architecture as ours** (ESP32-CAM MJPEG -> Python on a PC -> face_recognition/dlib or similar, e.g. the DEV Community weekend project, `jjateen/esp32-cam-security`, `h-enes-simsek/securityCam` with a Flask relay for several viewers); none used InsightFace, and on-device ESP32 face recognition is described as unsuitable for identification. Our pipeline (InsightFace on the laptop) is the stronger variant; the open problem is the pixels at the sensor.
Sources: arXiv 2505.24081; s60sc/ESP32-CAM_MJPEG2SD (UXGA at most about 10 fps when recording to SD, 20 MHz clock); Espressif/Arduino camera web server examples; Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 Sense documentation; Instructables ESP32-Cam M12 Lens Hack; JLCPCB ESP32-CAM guide.
