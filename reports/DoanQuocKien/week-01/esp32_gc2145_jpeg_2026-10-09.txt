ESP32-CAM camera diagnosis and software-JPEG speed, 2026-10-09 (DoanQuocKien)

1. First module: boot error 0x106 (core 3.x, KR's log) / 0x105 (core 2.0.17, PlatformIO espressif32@6.9.0). Cause: torn flat cable. Replaced.
2. Second module (bought locally): probes fine; "JPEG format is not supported on this sensor".
   Identified with esp32/sentry_cam (RGB565 + PID print): SENSOR PID=0x2145 = GC2145, I2C addr 0x3c. No hardware JPEG.
   (The Shopee listing said the sensor is random: OV3660, OV2640 or OV5640. It is none of them.)

6. Raw RGB565 over UDP to the laptop (esp32/raw_udp + tools/esp_raw_rx.py; QVGA 153,600 B/frame = 110 packets of 1400 B; the laptop finds the ESP32 by a HELO broadcast, no fixed IP)
   Network: phone hotspot, 2.4 GHz channel 13, laptop and ESP32 both on it (ESP32 RSSI -47 to -60 dBm).
   (First two attempts were invalid: the laptop's own Wi-Fi was on 5 GHz while its hotspot was on 2.4 GHz; one radio time-slices, ping to the ESP32 lost 95 %.)
   Camera on, 10 fps / 15 fps / as fast as possible: the ESP32 managed only 0.1-0.5 fps and dropped 5-43 packets per 2 s window; no frame completed on the laptop.
   Camera off (dummy data, same packets): 4.3-4.8 fps, 0 lost, 5.5-6.0 Mbit/s, first-to-last packet spread about 210 ms per frame,
   round trip time (frame sent -> ACK) 420-480 ms avg, laptop decode of RGB565 -> BGR about 1 ms, relative latency p95 about 55 ms.
   Conclusion: on this link the ESP32 carries about 6 Mbit/s of UDP, so raw QVGA tops out near 5 fps even with the camera off, and running the camera at the same time
   starves the sender. Raw streaming is worse than software JPEG at QVGA (7.7 fps, 25 KB/frame). VGA raw (614 KB/frame) is out of the question.

9. Tuning sweep (esp32/jpeg_udp, camera + encoder + Wi-Fi, as fast as possible, 7 s per setting, JPEG quality 60 unless stated). Encode time per frame, ESP32 side:
   QVGA dual-core (helper task on core 0)   57-62 ms     QVGA single task, 2 frame buffers   58-60 ms
   QVGA single task, 1 frame buffer          50-52 ms     QVGA 4:2:2 chroma instead of 4:2:0   66-67 ms (slower)
   QVGA quality 40                           57-59 ms     VGA dual-core                       275-288 ms
   Result: none of the knobs moves QVGA encode below about 50 ms; one frame buffer is about 10 ms faster, dual-core gives nothing once Wi-Fi is running.
   This run is NOT a fair fps comparison: the Wi-Fi link was worse (RSSI -55 -> -68 dBm), UDP sends failed with lwip error 12 (ENOMEM, 2,400+ log lines), camera overflow errors (EV-EOF-OVF, EV-VSYNC-OVF) appeared,
   and after about 50 s the camera could no longer be re-initialised ("DMA buffer 30720 Byte malloc failed, largest free block 30708 Byte": internal RAM exhausted/fragmented by repeated re-init).
   Lessons for the real firmware: initialise the camera once per boot (one resolution); never block on UDP sends (drop the frame when lwip is out of buffers) so latency stays bounded.

10. Research (web, 2026-10-09):
   - Others measure frame2jpg QVGA at RGB565 125 ms, GRAY 46 ms, YUV422 158 ms on ESP32 (espressif/esp32-camera issue 521): same as ours; no solution given there.
   - Espressif FAQ: sensors without JPEG need software JPEG on the ESP32 (esp-iot-solution pic_server).
   - esp32-camera README: 2+ frame buffers put more strain on CPU/memory; YUV/RGB strain PSRAM writes and can lose data with Wi-Fi on.
   - esp_new_jpeg benchmark pages give ESP32-S3 numbers only (1280x720 YCbYCr 4.84 fps); the classic ESP32 uses a plain build of the same library, as measured above.
   - A faster chip is the other way: an ESP32-S3 camera board (LCD_CAM interface, SIMD in esp_new_jpeg). Scaling the S3 figure by pixel count suggests about 12-15 fps at VGA. This is an estimate, not a measurement.

11. Second ESP32 board (the one sold with the camera; ESP32-D0WD-V3 rev 3.1, factory AT firmware before we flashed it), same camera module.
   Sensor ID read on it: PID 0x2145 = GC2145, address 0x3c. So the sensor is the same; the board is not the cause of the missing JPEG.
   Same test (esp32/jpeg_udp, QVGA color, dual-core encode): 11.5-12.2 fps for the first 3-4 windows (about 8 s), encode 58-61 ms, send 8 ms, then the same degradation as on the old board.
12. Degradation after a few seconds, both boards (esp32/jpeg_udp, one resolution, camera initialised once, phone hotspot 2.4 GHz channel 13, RSSI -55 to -64 dBm):
   send time per frame rises from 8 ms to 150-750 ms, send-fail and lwip errno 12 (ENOMEM) on thousands of packets, ESP32 ACK count drops to 0, laptop receives almost nothing; ping from laptop to the ESP32 returned "Destination host unreachable" (no ARP answer).
   Free internal heap stayed healthy: 70-105 KB free, largest block 43 KB (dual-core, 2 buffers) and 145-170 KB free, largest 74-98 KB (single task, 1 buffer). So it is not RAM exhaustion.
   Ruled out: memory, encoder settings, board. Open: phone hotspot behaviour on channel 13, or the ESP32 Wi-Fi TX path stalling. Next experiment: another 2.4 GHz channel (1, 6 or 11) or the laptop hotspot; keep frame-dropping in the final firmware.

13. Fixes found online and applied (esp32/jpeg_udp): HT20 channel width (esp_wifi_set_bandwidth), explicit country VN with channels 1-13 (esp_wifi_set_country), short retry then drop the frame instead of 20 x 1 ms retries (sketch no longer builds delay).
   Sources: Espressif Wi-Fi driver docs (HT40 default, HT20 recommended in crowded 2.4 GHz); esp32.com UDP ENOMEM threads (pace / drop); a Sunfounder FAQ claims ESP32 supports channels 1-11 only (not verified against Espressif docs).
   Result, phone hotspot still on channel 13: no more growing stalls (send 18-30 ms steady, no multi-second rtt), but the uplink still delivers almost nothing: 8-15 send failures per 2 s, ACKs about 0, laptop receives 0-3 fps.
   Not caused by: encoder settings, heap, board, 40 MHz width, country restriction. Remaining suspects: ESP32 uplink to the phone AP (power supply droop from laptop USB while camera + radio run, antenna selection resistor, phone AP behaviour), time-varying hotspot quality (the same hotspot gave 12 fps for about 8 s earlier, and the laptop-side Wi-Fi was fine: 144 Mbit/s).

14. Root cause search: why the Wi-Fi uplink dies while the camera runs (esp32/jpeg_udp A/B phases, QVGA JPEG about 7 KB/frame, phone hotspot ch 13, ESP32 powered from laptop USB; same packets in every phase; ESP32 side numbers per 2 s window)

   camera on + encoding, XCLK 20 MHz                     6.7-7 fps sent, 4-14 send failures, ACKs 0-6 of ~14, rtt 1-3 s
   camera on + encoding, XCLK 10 MHz                     3.5 fps, send failures 0-7, ACKs 1-5, rtt 1.6-3.8 s -> not the clock frequency
   camera on + encoding, TX power 19.5 / 11 / 5 dBm      all bad                                              -> not TX power / regulator sag
   camera on, frames grabbed, NO encoding                9.5-11 fps sent, 2-18 send failures, ACKs 0-19, rtt 0.8-1.6 s -> not the JPEG encoder or CPU load
   camera OFF (last JPEG resent at 11.5-12 fps)          0 send failures, 23-28 of ~24 ACKs per 2 s, rtt 21-24 ms, RSSI -39 to -45 dBm  <- perfect, repeated 3 times
   Also ruled out: free heap (70-240 KB), board (two ESP32 boards behave the same), HT40 width and country (fixed, no change).
   Conclusion: with the camera active (clock, pixel bus, ribbon cable, I2S DMA), the ESP32 cannot transmit reliably on 2.4 GHz; receive stays fine (RSSI normal). Same behaviour on both boards and with the same GC2145 camera module.
   Not yet tried: shielding the camera ribbon (aluminium foil/copper tape, grounded), a shorter ribbon or different camera module (OV2640), external IPEX antenna, a wired (USB/UART) link, pausing the camera between frames.

17. Can the GC2145 reach 640x480 at 15 fps? (old board, esp_new_jpeg, quality 60, 4:2:0)
   Encode only, camera off, no Wi-Fi, one real VGA YUV422 frame encoded 24 times (esp32/par_bench), frames per second:
     640x480  one encoder 5.1 | one encoder + helper task 7.7 | two encoders, one per core 5.9
     640x320 crop (top 320 rows): one 7.7 | helper 11.5 | two encoders 9.0
   Two parallel encoders are slower than the helper mode (shared memory bandwidth). Ceiling for full VGA on a classic ESP32: about 7.7 fps before Wi-Fi uses any CPU.
   Real stream, VGA, camera + dual-core encode + Wi-Fi, old board, 54 s: ESP32 sent 1.7-3.1 fps, encode 221-228 ms, 22 KB/frame (16 packets), 1-5 send failures per 2 s, laptop received 0.9 fps average, 63 frames lost.
   Conclusion: 640x480 at 15 fps is not reachable with this camera on this chip. Options: OV2640 module (hardware JPEG; others report about 29 fps VGA on the AI-Thinker board, not measured by us) or an ESP32-S3 board (SIMD; estimate only).
   Web research found no classic-ESP32 VGA encoder benchmark and no hardware JPEG mode for the GC2145 (Espressif FAQ: use software JPEG).

18. Software route to 640x480 on the GC2145 (old board, esp32/tile_stream + tools/esp_tile_rx.py), 2026-10-09 night
   Findings, in the order they were found:
   a) The JPEG encoder is CPU-bound, not memory-bound: strips/tiles copied into internal RAM give the same 198-218 ms per VGA frame (esp32/par_bench). Cost is about 0.65 us per pixel (YUV 4:2:0) whatever the tile size, so encoding only changed tiles scales with the changed area.
   b) The camera, not the encoder, limited VGA: YUV422 640x480 delivers only 5.0 fps at XCLK 20 MHz (2.5 fps at 10 MHz; 320x240 gives 13.5 fps) (esp32/cam_rate).
      The stock driver deliberately throttles the sensor pixel clock for the ESP32 (0xf8 low 6 bits = divx4, 0xfa high nibble = divide_by; comment "pclk <= 15MHz"). Halving the divider made frames arrive truncated (FB-SIZE 499200 != 614400): the ESP32 camera input cannot take 2 bytes/pixel that fast.
   c) Raw Bayer halves the data (1 byte/pixel). The ESP32 camera layer rejects PIXFORMAT_RAW ("Requested format is not supported", divide by zero), and the GC2145 driver only knows YUV422/RGB565, but it only counts bytes per frame:
      480x320 YUV422 = 307200 B = one 640x480 raw frame. So: init as HVGA YUV422, then s->set_framesize(VGA), set_reg(0xfe,0) and set_reg(0x84, 0x17) (raw output). Frames then arrive as 307200 B with no errors. Verified as a real picture (BG Bayer pattern) with tools/esp_grab_raw.py.
   d) Raw capture rate vs divider (Wi-Fi off): divx4 2 = 5.0 fps, 3 = 6.7, 4 = 8.3, 5 = 10.0, 6 = 11.7, 7 and up = 13.0 (a ceiling; divide_by 1 corrupts frames above about 12.7 fps).
   e) Wi-Fi slows the camera: Wi-Fi off 13.0 fps; connected idle 7.7; sending 100 pkt/s 6.7; 300 pkt/s 4.0; with hundreds of cam_hal EV-EOF-OVF overflow errors. The camera interrupt lands on the core that calls esp_camera_init: camera started on core 0 (the Wi-Fi core) holds 12.0-12.7 fps with Wi-Fi connected, started on core 1 only 8-11.8 fps. The stream therefore runs as a task pinned to core 0.
      (Probably the same interaction that broke the Wi-Fi uplink in sections 12-15 on the second board; not verified.)
   f) Conditional replenishment: the camera is fixed, so only changed 64x32 tiles are encoded (grey JPEG of the raw Bayer tile, quality 80) and sent, biggest change first, within a time budget; the two oldest tiles are always refreshed so packet loss heals.
      Change detection uses a fingerprint per tile in internal RAM (8 rows x 16 bytes); threshold 16 (raw Bayer is noisy: threshold 6 flagged 94 of 150 tiles in a still scene). Scoring all 150 tiles takes about 5 ms.
      The JPEG work runs on a worker task on core 1, results are packed several tiles per UDP packet (a UDP send costs about 3.5 ms whatever its size). The JPEG header is sent once a second; tiles carry only scan data.
   Result, full 640x480 picture on the laptop (tools/esp_tile_rx.py, demosaic BG), 64 s run, phone hotspot ch 13, rssi -47:
      still scene: 10-12 fps, 2 tiles per frame, 0.1 Mbit/s, tile decode 0.1 ms, no missing tiles
      large movement (100 dirty tiles): 6-9 fps with 20-27 tiles per frame, 0.3-0.5 Mbit/s, a few missing tiles (healed by the refresh)
      encoder throughput under load: about 160 tiles per second (about 2 ms per 64x32 tile on core 1)
   Limits: assumes a fixed camera (a shaking or moving camera makes every tile dirty); raw colours have no white balance or gamma yet (blue cast); the ceiling is 13 fps from the camera interface, and large moving regions update at about 5 fps.
   Ideas not yet tried: 640x360 window (230 KB/frame, about 17 fps capture); quality 60 (faster encode); shrinking scoring cost; white balance on the laptop; splitting the Bayer mosaic into 4 planes before JPEG (smoother, cheaper).

19. 640x480 at 15 fps reached (old board, esp32/tile_stream, Wi-Fi on, phone hotspot ch 13)
   Cause of the 13 fps wall: above xclk 10 MHz the esp32-camera driver uses I2S "high-speed" sampling (SM_0A00_0B00): every sensor byte occupies a 4-byte DMA word and cam_task filters them out one by one.
   At xclk <= 10 MHz it uses SM_0A0B_0C0D (2 bytes per word, half the DMA traffic and half the filter work). The threshold is only a proxy for pixel speed; the GC2145 PLL restores the pixel rate.
   Blanking (Wi-Fi off, esp32/cam_rate): vblank 46 -> 12 lines: 12.7 -> 13.3 fps at xclk 20; vblank 4 or any hblank below 280: frames corrupted (FB-SIZE).
   xclk 10 MHz, vblank 12: divide_by 2 plateaus at 13.3 fps for divx4 15-29 (sensor limit); divide_by 1: divx4 9 = 13.0, divx4 11 = 15.7 fps clean, divx4 13 = 8.7 (frames dropped).
   Tile stream with xclk 10 MHz, divx4 11, divide_by 1, vblank 12, camera on core 0, Wi-Fi on: ESP32 13.2-15.6 fps (frame interval 64-76 ms), 0 FB-SIZE errors, 9 overflow lines in 40 s;
   laptop 13-18 fps (mostly 14-16) at full 640x480, 0.1-0.4 Mbit/s, round trip 86-200 ms. Mostly still scene: 2-9 tiles per frame. Large motion not yet measured with these settings (encode+send about 2.2 ms per tile, so heavy motion lowers the rate).

20. Motion test of the 15 fps tile stream (same settings as section 19, budget 55 ms, quality 80, 72 s, person moving in front of the camera; laptop side, per second)
   still (dirty < 3 tiles)      5 s: 12.4 fps, 2.7 tiles/frame, 0 missing
   small motion (3-10 dirty)   14 s: 10.5 fps (min 4), 7.6 tiles/frame, 0.2 missing/s, 0.25 Mbit/s
   moderate (10-40 dirty)      19 s:  9.3 fps (min 2), 15.3 tiles/frame, 1.5 missing/s, 0.39 Mbit/s
   large motion (>= 40 dirty)  30 s:  7.3 fps (min 2), 20.4 tiles/frame, 10.4 missing/s, 0.45 Mbit/s
   ESP32 side in heavy motion: encode+send 58-85 ms per frame (about 2.8 ms per tile), 2-22 send failures per 2 s, round trip 0.3-1.0 s, camera still 0 FB-SIZE errors.
   So the 15 fps figure holds for still / slight motion; with a person moving across the frame the stream drops to 7-10 fps and starts losing tiles (UDP uplink strain; refresh repairs them).
   Next: smaller per-frame budget (35 ms, so the loop keeps up with the 15 fps camera), quality 60, planar Bayer split for cheaper JPEG, lower per-tile send cost.
